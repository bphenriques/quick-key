# Agent Instructions

A Quickshell menu and its home-manager module. What it does is in [README.md](./README.md) and the docs. This file is the rules.

## Design rules

Every change follows these; a change that cannot is a reason to revisit the rule here first, not to work around it.

1. **Data, not code.** What the menu shows and does comes from `menu.entries`. QML has no case for a particular entry, tool or device; a new need becomes a setting in `Config.qml`, with a check in `Validation.qml` when it can be wrong. A built-in (status, list, state, action) is one entry in its table, never a `case` or name test elsewhere.
2. **Tools through the config.** QML runs nothing by name. The config that owns a tool sets its command, and a default may name only a standard tool (`systemd-inhibit`, `notify-send`, Wayland's `wl-paste`), or, in a plugin named after a tool, that tool: listing the plugin is choosing it. The shell reads only output it defines (the state line, see [Sources](docs/src/plugins.md#sources)), never a tool's own format. What needs more of one tool is a plugin. The compositor is reached only through `Compositor` (see [Compositor](docs/src/compositor.md)). The one exception: a link opens with the desktop's handler for links.
3. **Events over polling.** What has no events is read when the menu opens.
4. **Layout is declared.** A level is its body over an optional bar, the bar at the screen's edge. The top level's bar is `menu.header`; an entry may declare its own `header` with the same slots. A slot holds entries, drawn as their status or else their icon, and statuses and plugins by name; a bar entry may do nothing but show its status, and one with both an icon and a status is its icon, taking the status's tone (of what its `format` shows), a spinner while it is busy and its value as the tip, the value beside it too while that tone warns. A body holds one thing: a menu's entries on their rows (the top level, a level without a list or view, an item's own menu), or a level's content, its list or its view (`calendar`). A content level's actions, drawn as their rows, then its legend sit on its bar's left, before its header's left slot, so a list that grows moves neither; a level with nothing for its bar is its body alone. Nothing divides content from actions; a divider only splits content (the calendar's month from its agenda). A level's width comes from its entry (`width`, else `menu.width`) and its columns from its entry's `columns`, else `menu.columns`, never measured from what it shows, so it holds still; entries flow across, so order decides neighbours. At the top the columns are equal, so values line up; in a level the last hugs its rows against the right edge. Make the menu wider before adding columns or truncating. The menu and a plugin's panel (Ask) share one frame, `Panel`: the same place, the bar at the screen's edge, the body over it and an optional input, a level's island over its body and a plugin's prompt under its own (Ask's conversation), one open at a time. A plugin's panel's bar holds its own legend on the left and the top level's right slot on the right. An entry's `input` floats over its level's body, a bar entry's too, and `menu.input` over the top level's, and writes: its text, in its `format`, goes to its `cmd`, else its `plugin` or the plugin the level lists, `after` running once it is done. Config says where text goes; how the write is done (order, retries, reporting) is the command's, never a new field. An input's island is the working area, what you type and what its plugin says about it (`reply`), and the body the committed area, what happened: the level redrawing is the proof. A conversation is yours as bubbles on the right and the other's as plain text on the left, never glyphs; the island holds the last exchange from the moment it is sent, a line each at first, growing away from the edge and never shrinking within the visit. An exchange draws its `phase`: working (a spinner and what it is doing), answered, choosing (options a digit picks), waiting on you, failed (alert, the text given back), stopped; the prompt says what `RET` does next (ask, reply, pick). An answer the island cannot hold (past three lines, a table, options that do not fit one line, something waiting on you) opens the plugin's own panel by itself, as a popup does, and `ESC` comes back; `TAB` to the exchange and `RET`, or a click, opens it too, and `y` copies its answer, in the chat too, where a bare letter is otherwise its Ctrl chord: allowing takes `C-y` only, so a key learned as harmless never approves; what the prompt cannot run (a send that failed) is faded, and what you answered on the way (an approval) stays as a dim line. An answer is Markdown, as ACP sends it, and streams in as the agent writes it: the conversation renders it, the island shows it plain; its structure is the Markdown's own (a table, a closing numbered list as the options), never guessed from plain text. An input never filters. An agent's sits on a level whose data it changes, and once at the top level as the catch-all for what belongs to no level: never one on every level.
5. **Order is explicit.** Top level by `lib.mkOrder`, rows pairing related things. Within a level, the list order, paired for the row it shares.
6. **Labels.** Sentence case; `...`, the one ellipsis (`Words`), when it opens a picker or an app, and on what is under way or waits on you (`syncing...`, `Reply...`); a pair named for what it changes (`Volume`, `Track`). A row's label is its `desc` and never changes; what its status reports sits on its right (rule 8). A bar holds content (a song, its modes), never a title; only an app's menu, which no entry names, names itself there (`blueman › Plugins`). Device levels put Power on `p` and Configure... on `c`; every row of a level carries an icon (an `action` brings its own, showing its state: Wi-Fi off, Bluetooth on); `space` is only ever a picker. What ends the session takes Shift (Reboot, Shutdown).
7. **Keys hold still.** Letters are actions and digits are list items. Ctrl reaches the bar and Alt an item's own menu, and they compose: in the body `1` runs an item and `M-1` opens its menu, in the bar `C-1` and `C-M-1`; a bar entry declares its key plain (`c`) and takes it with Ctrl (`C-c`). Own menus keep letters from the labels their apps supply. Holding Ctrl a second lists the bar's keys in place of the body, each named and the body keeping its height, as icons alone cannot say what they run; holding Super, each bar entry's `shortcut`, its compositor key; `?` lists in the same way the keys no row shows (the holds, `M-digit`, `ESC`, an input's `TAB` and `C-S-RET`), any key closing it. A list shows up to nine rows on fixed digits; `/` filters it in place unless its level binds `/` itself (the `pick` action filters on any key), the legend saying `/ 1-9 of 14` while it holds more, as in a pager or a browser, the filter taking the level's island and the letters, the one place where digits follow the rows shown, `↑↓` the cursor, `RET` the item under it and `M-RET` its own menu. A plugin's panel opens a list filtered on a level of its own (`Picker`), as a bar's `+N` does. An item's own menu is its `actions`, data from its list (a tray app's is the app's); other pickers take any free key (`.` or a letter), their labels ending in `...`. A body key may carry Ctrl or Alt in its name where its bar does not take it (`Ctrl+Left`, as niri writes bindings); hints show keys Emacs-style, `C-` Ctrl, `M-` Alt, `s-` Super, Shift as the capital letter (`C-←→`, `s-k`), symbols for the arrows, `DEL` for Backspace and `ESC` for Escape as Emacs writes them; a modifier never runs the plain key, Shift only picks the capital letter, and Super stays the compositor's. An input takes letters only while focused, so keys hold still: `TAB` moves the keys from the body to its input, on every level and in Ask, as Tab moves between areas anywhere, and `TAB` or `S-TAB` moves them back (without an input, both are Qt's); a click focuses it too. `RET` sends and keeps it focused, the answer above it, and while you stay on the level the next send continues the same conversation (leaving or closing ends it), `C-S-RET` sends and closes the surface, in Ask too (the answer arrives as a notification); the whole conversation is Ask's, opened by its own key or a click on the answer. A choice waiting in an answer takes the digits while the input is empty, a digit picking and never typing; a letter first types, digits too. A legend shows a content level's actions and the keys the state changes, each while it applies; the other fixed keys are `?`'s, in Ask too while its prompt is dim. `ESC` is the one way back, as in a launcher: it clears what is being typed, then closes what is open on top (the filter, a focused event), then returns where you came from (the level above, or the panel or input that opened it, focused again), closing at the root (the top level, or a level or panel opened on its own); it never moves the keys, stops or answers anything, and a legend names it only while it clears. `DEL` only deletes. `C-c` stops a run, refusing what it waits to be allowed. A level's input starts waiting, Ask's focused, being what it opens for; waiting, a panel's own letters act (Ask's `y`, `l`, `o`, `r`, `c`, also `C-` while typing). `C-r` in an input lists its plugin's past conversations, filtered (`recall`), as a shell's reverse search does, in Ask too. A key path (`a 2`) is what a binding runs (`menu press`), so a binding never drifts from the menu. Pinned items keep their digit and dim while absent, and their label names them wherever the menu shows them; the rest follow by name (internal audio first), never by signal or recency.
8. **State is visible.** The menu shows state, the OSD shows changes. A row's status and a listed item's detail sit on its right as data, values in a column; an action's value in its level, alone in reporting, follows its label instead. It is the status's short value, or the entry's `format` over its fields, shortened when it does not fit. A level's state sits on the row that changes it: a device's volume beside it, brightness on Brightness, power in Power's icon. Tone goes to what reports, never to a label beside it: a listed item in use and a toggle that is on (`activeWhen`) take `primary`, and so does the in-use item's value, the item in use bold as well so colour is not all that says so; a value's tone is its own (`warning` on a late count, beside its alarm), and a field shown through a `format` may carry one (`warning` on a sync older than `staleAfter`). The right of a row never holds a word for a state its icon or tone already shows; a status names its state only when it has no value to show (`off`, `muted`, `paused`). An empty list says why: off, or nothing found (a plugin through its `empty`); a level whose source failed says why beside its keys, in `alert`. What cannot run right now dims, so keys hold still; what can never run on this machine is not declared. Routine state may wait on hover: a bar icon's value is its tip and its spinner says it is under way; when it needs you, its value shows beside it in its tone (a stale sync's `updated 13:02`), so colour is never all that says so. A listed item that is done (a ticked task) dims too, struck through, and still runs: struck, a dim row is finished, not unavailable. One done before the visit is `hidden`: off the rows, still found by filtering (`/`), so what is finished never buries what is left. A listed item's value may take a tone of its own for a state that needs attention, its label staying plain (`warning` on a late task's date), and a glyph before it, so colour is never all that says so (a late task's alarm). A listed item still being made (a task being added) shows a spinner in place of its icon, as an input does in its glyph's place while its plugin takes no text (Ask's turn); a failure says so in alert tone with its glyph and gives the text back, so `RET` retries; an input's unsent text stays until sent or cleared, as Ask's does.
9. **Text is plain, in one font at one size.** What stands out does so by tone, weight and space, never by size. Bold (`strong`) is what to find first: a heading, the clock, the item in use, an answer's emphasis; keys are told by their tone, so they stay regular. A heading is bold at the body size wherever it comes from (the calendar's month, an answer's `##`). Wrapped prose (an answer) is spaced at `theme.lineHeight`; rows keep their own padding. Everything renders through `Label`, which never interprets markup: titles from apps and streams are untrusted. An agent's answer is the one exception (it is the agent you configured): `Markup` renders its Markdown as rich text of the kit's own making, the agent's text escaped, so it fetches nothing and runs no HTML, and draws its parts in the menu's terms: a table's header bold over one divider and its columns apart, never a grid; code on a faint wash, in the one font; a quote dim behind a rule. The one source-specific case in the core is next to it: Music reads song and artist out of the XML some radio streams (Bauer Media) send as their title, since MPRIS leaves no glue to do it in.
10. **Done means seen working.** A change is tested where it runs (see [DEVELOPMENT.md](DEVELOPMENT.md)), not only evaluated.
11. **Widgets compose the kit.** Colour and dimming come from a `tone`, glyphs go through `Icon`, the wheel through `WheelSteps`, a colour that is data (a calendar's) through `Swatch`, a size in characters or lines through `Metrics`, outlines and dividers through `Surface`, `Pill` and `Divider` (the theme's `border` and `borderWidth`, a pill's tone only for a state), emphasis through `Label`'s `strong`, a surface the keyboard opens through `Panel`, the words a state or a key is named by (a legend's, a prompt's, the ellipsis) through `Words`, a date or a time through `Clock` (`time`, `day`, `when`, `recent`, `heading`), in `clock.locale`; a widget never sets opacity to dim, picks a theme colour or size itself, or draws a glyph as text.
12. **The core is what every desktop has.** Audio, network, Bluetooth, power, outputs and backlight, tray, music, the calendar. Anything tied to one tool or taste is a plugin: off until listed, reaching the shell only through its API (see [Plugins](docs/src/plugins.md)), and held to these rules like the core, keyboard first and built from the kit.
13. **Motion confirms, never delays.** Surfaces fade in and out, colours ease, all over `theme.animationDuration` (`0` for none) and through the kit (`Fade`, `Label`, `Icon`), never a widget's own; a highlight follows the pointer at once, as easing trails it. Nothing waits on an animation: a key acts at once, the menu appears and closes at once (its background blur cannot fade with it, and a fade-out would hold the keyboard), and nothing moves or resizes: a conversation's island grows with its turns away from the edge, never shrinking within a visit, so nothing else moves, and opening the conversation is a change of surface, as opening a level is.

## Layout

The shell, under `packages/quick-key/`:

- `services/`: state read from its source (D-Bus, Pipewire, niri, commands), pushed by events. What a service declares at its top level is its API, read-only where it is state; what it keeps for itself sits in an object under an `id`, which no other file can reach. Sources without events (the outputs, plugins' `read` commands) are read on `refresh()`, which the menu calls as it opens. `Plugin` holds each plugin's one source (`watch`, `read`, `file`, through `CommandState`) and its IPC target.
- `ui/`: the kit ([Kit](docs/src/kit.md)). Widgets use it and the theme from `Config`, never raw colours or sizes.
- `builtins/`: the built-in names, one table each (`Statuses`, `Lists`, `States`, `Actions`): what a status shows and its fields, what a list holds and does, what lights a toggle, what an entry's `action` runs. Names are camelCase in every table.
- `widgets/`: the surfaces. `menu/` splits into `MenuModel` (levels, their bars and keys) and its view (`Menu`, `MenuHeader` with its `HeaderSlot`s and `HeaderItem`s for a bar, `MenuRow`, `MenuStatus`); `menu/calendar/` holds a calendar level (`CalendarModel`, `CalendarMonth`, `CalendarEvents`). `ListFilter` filters a level's list in place. A surface with state of its own keeps it in a `*Model`, the view only reading it.
- `plugins/`: the plugins the shell ships; `Plugin.qml` is their root, `PluginSettings.qml` their settings' root, `services/Plugins.qml` loads them. A plugin is a folder holding all of it: `<Name>.qml` (its state and actions, never drawing), `Settings.qml` (every setting it takes), and its views, listed in `views` and created with it, since a plugin folder is not a module the shell imports.
- `themes/`: the shipped colour presets, read through `ThemeFile.qml`.
- `menu.json`: the starter menu, shaped as a `config.json` so users can copy it.

The rest:

- `packages/calendar-sync/`: writes `calendar.file` from a list of calendars (vdirsyncer, khal); the shell never runs it.
- `packages/preview/`: runs a working tree in a nested niri (see [DEVELOPMENT.md](DEVELOPMENT.md)).
- `tests/`: the flake checks beyond the builds (see [DEVELOPMENT.md](DEVELOPMENT.md)).
- `modules/home-manager/quick-key/`: the module. `default.nix` runs the shell and writes `settings` as given to `config.json`, which must pass the shell's check as the configuration builds; `calendar-sync.nix` runs calendar-sync.

`Config.qml` is the schema of `config.json`: each key, its kind and its default. What a schema cannot say (an entry's fields, a name that must exist) is a check in `Validation.qml`, which the shell logs as it starts and `check.qml` fails on. Nothing else lists the settings or the built-in names.

## Code style

- Idiomatic Nix and QML. YAGNI. Read neighbouring files and match their patterns.
- A Repeater over a list that changes takes its length as `model`, each delegate reading its item by `index`, `?? ({})` since rows past a shrunk list's end read it before they go: an array model recreates every delegate on each change, dropping hover and wheel state. A list that changes many times a second, each item costly to draw (a streaming answer's pieces), keeps a `ListModel` in step instead, so only the item that changed is told (`Markdown`).
- Nushell logic in a `script.nu`, wrapped by `writeNushellApplication` (`packages/builders.nix`).
- One-line `description` on every option, `defaultText` whenever a `default` references other config. Detail goes in a docs chapter.

## Comments

- Default to none. Comment only what the code can't say: the why, a non-obvious constraint, a cross-file pointer.
- One line, never two in a row (`tests/comments.nix` fails the check). Never restate code or repeat what the docs say.

## Writing style (docs, README)

- Plain declaratives, point first. No windup.
- No em-dashes.
- No LLM cadence: no "not just X but Y", no padded threes, no restating a point.
- Say it once. Cut every word the sentence survives without.

## Docs

- `nix build .#docs` builds the mdBook in `docs/`. Chapters in `docs/src/`, ordered by `SUMMARY.md`. `docs.nix` generates the reference: the settings as `Config.qml` declares them, then the module's options. Preview with `mdbook serve docs`.
- Chapters explain the model, never the options.
- Update the chapter in the same change as the behaviour.
